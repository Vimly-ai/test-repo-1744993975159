# Cold Call Script - Website Sales for Treasure Valley Businesses

## THE MINDSET
You're not selling. You're helping. These businesses are losing customers every day
because people can't find them online. You already did the work - you built them
something. You're just letting them know it exists.

Sound casual, friendly, like a local kid who genuinely wants to help. NOT a salesperson.

---

## OPENER #1 - "I Already Built It" (Best for HOT LEADS with NO website)

> "Hey, is this [Business Name]? Cool - my name is [Your Name]. So this might
> sound a little random, but I was looking for [their service - like 'a plumber
> in Nampa'] the other day and I noticed you guys don't really have a website.
> I actually build websites for local businesses around the Treasure Valley, so
> I went ahead and put one together for you guys just to show you what it could
> look like. Where can I send it over for you to check out?"

**If they say "send it to my email":**
> "Awesome, what's the best email? I'll shoot it over right now. No pressure at
> all - just wanted you to see it. If you like it, cool, we can talk. If not,
> no worries."

**If they ask "how much?":**
> "Honestly it depends on what you want, but we keep it really affordable for
> local businesses. Can I send over what I built first so you can see it? Then
> we can chat about it."

**If they seem skeptical:**
> "I totally get it. I'm not trying to sell you anything right now. I just
> build sites for local companies and I thought you guys could use one. Take
> a look and if it's not for you, no hard feelings."

---

## OPENER #2 - "Mom/Family Used You" (Best for businesses WITH a janky website)

> "Hey, is this [Business Name]? Hey my name is [Your Name]. So my
> [mom/dad/uncle/neighbor] actually used you guys [recently/last month] and
> they said you were awesome. But when they were trying to find you online it
> was kind of tough. I actually build websites for local businesses, and I
> went ahead and made you guys a new one just to see if you'd be interested.
> Where can I send it for you to take a look?"

**If they ask who your mom/family member is:**
> "It was a while back so I don't remember all the details, but they had
> good things to say. Anyway, the main reason I'm calling is about the
> website - can I shoot it over to you?"

---

## OPENER #3 - "Google Search" (Good for businesses ranking poorly)

> "Hey is this [Business Name]? My name is [Your Name]. I was searching
> for [plumbers/electricians/etc] in [Boise/Nampa/etc] and I found you guys
> but you were pretty far down the list. I build websites for local
> businesses that help them show up higher on Google, and I actually put
> something together for you. Can I send it over?"

---

## HANDLING COMMON RESPONSES

### "We're not interested"
> "Totally fine, I appreciate your time. If you ever change your mind, I'm
> local - just a kid in the Treasure Valley trying to help out local
> businesses. Have a good one!"

### "We already have a website"
> "Oh yeah I saw that! I actually think yours could be doing a lot better
> on Google though. I made an updated version that's more modern and would
> help you show up higher when people search for [their service]. Can I
> send it over just so you can compare?"

### "How much does it cost?"
> "It really depends on what you need, but we work with local businesses
> so we keep it reasonable. Nothing crazy. Can I send over what I built
> first? That way you can see if you even like it before we talk numbers."

### "Who do you work for?"
> "My dad and I run a local agency here in the Treasure Valley. We do
> websites and help local businesses show up on Google. We're not some
> big company - just local guys helping local businesses."

### "Send me some info"
> "For sure - what's the best email? I'll send over the website I built
> for you guys and our info. What's a good time to follow up - like
> Thursday or Friday?"

### "I need to talk to my partner/wife/business partner"
> "Absolutely, I totally get that. Let me send it over so you both can
> look at it together. What email should I use? And when's a good time
> for me to check back in?"

### "We get calls like this all the time"
> "Yeah I bet you do, and most of them are from some company in another
> state. I'm literally right here in [Boise/the Treasure Valley]. I'm
> not trying to lock you into anything - I just built you a site and
> wanted to show you. Two minutes to look at it, that's all I'm asking."

## IMPORTANT TIPS

1. **SMILE when you talk** - people can hear it in your voice
2. **Stand up while calling** - you'll sound more energetic
3. **Keep it casual** - you're a local kid, not a telemarketer
4. **Don't oversell** - the goal is to GET THEIR EMAIL and send the site
5. **Always set a follow-up** - "Can I check back Thursday?"
6. **Take notes** - write down who you talked to, what they said, when to call back
7. **Best times to call** - Tuesday through Thursday, 9-11am or 2-4pm
8. **Don't call Monday mornings** - everyone's slammed
9. **If you get voicemail:**
   > "Hey this is [Your Name], I'm a local web guy in the Treasure Valley.
   > I put together a website for [Business Name] and wanted to show it to
   > you. Give me a call back at [your number] or I'll try you again in a
   > couple days. Thanks!"

---

## TRACKING YOUR CALLS

For each call, write down:
- Business name
- Who you talked to (get their name!)
- What they said
- Email if they gave one
- When to follow up
- Rate the call: Hot (interested) / Warm (maybe) / Cold (not interested)

---

## CALL ORDER PRIORITY

Start with rows 1-23 in the spreadsheet (HOT LEADS) - these businesses
have NO website or are using a free template. They NEED this the most
and are most likely to say yes.

Then move to rows 24-39 (HIGH priority) - outdated or broken websites.

Then rows 40-150 (MEDIUM) - basic websites that could use an upgrade.
